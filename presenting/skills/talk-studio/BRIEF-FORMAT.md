# BRIEF.md Format

`BRIEF.md` grounds every decision about the talk. It is short and rewritten when the picture changes (the change is logged in `DECISIONS.md`).

## Structure

```md
# {Talk title}

**One line:** {What the talk is, for whom.}
**Stakes level:** casual | standard | high-stakes

## Audience
- Who: {roles, size, mix}. Who may join later: {if a series}.
- What they already know and do: {baseline}.
- What they care about: {concerns, motivations}.

## Purpose
- After the talk they should think: {…}
- After the talk they should do: {…}
- Success looks like: {observable signal: questions asked, a practice adopted, a follow-up booked}.

## Format
- Duration: {minutes}. Type: talk | conversation | workshop | mixed.
- Tone: {e.g. friends around a table, formal, persuasive}.
- Venue and medium: {in person, online, recorded}. Interruptions: {welcome | Q&A at end}.

## The speaker
- Role and credibility on this topic: {…}
- Their story: {the personal experience that can carry the talk}.
- Preferences: {visual style, how much text on slides, language}.

## Constraints
- Confidentiality: {what must not leave the workspace or appear on slides}.
- Must include / must avoid: {…}
- Deadline: {date of the talk}.
```

## Rules

- **Purpose is about the audience,** not the speaker. "Show our research" is not a purpose; "leave aware that X and able to start Y" is.
- **Record the speaker's story** even if it seems small. It is usually the spine.
- **Keep the audience as wide as the user says it is.** If the talk is part of a series for everyone in a field, write that, so later sections and tables don't narrow to the first session's attendees.
