# FLOW.md Format

`FLOW.md` is the plan of the talk in the order it will be performed. The speaker reads it to imagine themselves on stage and to prepare; they do not read from it on stage. Every fact in it comes from `FACTS.md`.

## Structure

```md
# {Talk title}: plan

{One line: what this is (a talk, a conversation, a workshop) and what it is for.}
Details and sources: `FACTS.md`, `research/`.

**Duration:** {minutes} · **Tone:** {from BRIEF} · **Purpose:** {from BRIEF}
**Spine:** {The one story that opens and closes the talk, and where the thesis is said.}
**Rhythm:** story/question → one idea → at most one piece of evidence → conversation → bridge.

---

## {N}. {Section title} ({start}–{end} min)

**What we are doing:** {One sentence: the purpose of this section in the arc.}

**Opening:** {How the section opens: a story, a show of hands, a question, a callback.}

**What is said:**
- {The idea, in the speaker's voice, in the order it unfolds.}
- **{Name of a study, organisation or person}:** {One-line background: who or what, and why credible.}
  {What they found or said, with the number or quote from FACTS.md.}
- **To be fair:** {The limits of the finding, when it has any.}
- {Examples, a worked illustration, a short code or format sample when the audience needs to see the shape of something.}

**Slide {k}, {short name}:** {Exactly what the slide shows: chart type and values, the quote with attribution, the diagram. Source line. "Illustrative" if anonymised.}

**Conversation:** "{The question to the room.}" → {What to do with the answers: note them, come back to them in section X.}

**Bridge:** "{The sentence that carries the room into the next section.}"

---

## Closing ({start}–{end} min)

{Callback to the opening story ("how would I do it today?"), the takeaways as concrete steps, then the thesis said once, alone.}

**Slide {k}:** {Thesis only.}

## Open conversation ({start}–{end} min)

{Prompts to open the floor; a hook for the next session if there is one.}

---

## If asked (not in the talk)

| Question | Short answer | Details |
|---|---|---|
| {Likely question} | {Two or three sentences, with the number if one is needed} | {research/NNNN or FACTS id} |

## Slides ({count})

1. {name} · 2. {name} · …

## Time flexibility

- Buffer: {minutes} for interruptions and conversation.
- If time runs short: {which section shrinks first, then which item is cut}.
- Do not cut: {the heart-of-the-conversation section}.

## Revisions

- {YYYY-MM-DD}: {what changed and why}
```

## Rules

- **Order of performance, not topic grouping.** Each section exists because of where it sits in the arc.
- **Every section has all six blocks** (what we are doing, opening, what is said, slide, conversation, bridge). Opening and closing sections may drop the slide or the conversation; say so.
- **Background for every name.** No study, organisation or person appears without a line saying who or what they are.
- **Numbers only from `FACTS.md`,** with their confidence respected: vendor data as signal, contested incidents as "reported".
- **The thesis appears once,** in the closing, unless the brief asks for a statement up front (some high-stakes formats do; record that in `DECISIONS.md`).
- **One heart.** Mark one conversation as the main one and protect it in *Time flexibility*.
- **High-stakes talks** add, per section, a *Rehearsal* line (what to practise, the transition to land, the likely interruption) and an objection map after *If asked*.
- **Confidential material** is anonymised in the slide description and marked illustrative.
